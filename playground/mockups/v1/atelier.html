Here is the case for each, plainly.

Keep them together (pgvector-on-SQLite). Your store is already encrypted at the column level, already on the rotate-key endpoint, already migrated through ten versions without an outage. A second store is a second backup story, a second permission seam, a second thing to be wrong about when the laptop is on a train and the wifi has the disposition of a sulking cat.

Bring a specialty in (LanceDB). When the agent reaches a few hundred thousand embeddings, the SQLite query planner stops being your friend, and a dedicated index — columnar, IVF-flat, persisted to a single directory — will out-perform the integrated approach by an order of magnitude on the kinds of queries you'd actually run. Your encryption story is still tractable: LanceDB can live inside the same volume; the rotate-key endpoint extends to it with one careful afternoon.

For an essay you're writing for a friend, write the integrated answer. For an essay you'll need to defend at scale, write the second.

Would you like me to draft the first half of the piece — the case for integration, written for your keyboard friend — and leave the second open for the rebuttal you'll get from her?
